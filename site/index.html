03 / A HIDDEN SHORTCUTAppearance-stripping analysis

Did memory remember?
Or did the prompt tell it?

A character can return after a long gap even with no visual memory, because the current prompt describes the character’s appearance.

On the same 62 long-gap character-return events, removing the appearance information sharply reduces no-memory presence. Long-gap success alone is insufficient evidence of visual-memory use.

Targeted prompt intervention; these are entity-presence rates, not proof of identity fidelity or a memory-capacity scaling law.

05 / WHAT TO TAKE AWAYFor builders and evaluators

Ask three questions
before you retrieve.

01

What information
is valid now?

Historical fidelity is useful only when the remembered state still agrees with the current story.

02

What else comes
with that reference?

A needed entity may share its frame with a forbidden character, a look-alike or an obsolete appearance.

03

What did the prompt
already supply?

Separate gains from visual history from successes explained by the current prompt itself.

What the study establishes, and what remains open

The main comparison measures complete retrieval policies on one shared generator, conditional on one rollout per story. Histories diverge, so it does not isolate the causal effect of replacing a single reference. The paper also reports second-judge and eight-story H3 sensitivity checks. Absolute rates depend on the judge, and the backend check has limited scope. Last-seen has privileged gold-ID access and variable reference counts; it is not a deployable method or a budget-matched upper bound. The 224-unit mechanism audit was performed by one author and does not estimate population failure frequencies.
